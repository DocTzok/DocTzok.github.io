About

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin, working in the Georgakopoulos-Soares Lab. My research sits where generative AI meets genomics: I study what genomic language models actually learn, where they fall short of producing realistic DNA, and how to make these models faster, more interpretable, and more reliable in biomedical settings.

Before UT Austin, I earned my M.Eng. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the National Technical University of Athens, with a thesis on deep learning for 3D brain tumor segmentation in MRI. Along the way I designed a hierarchical VAE for polymer discovery at Fujitsu Research in Tokyo (Vulcanus in Japan program), spent a semester at the Technical University of Denmark, and did wet-lab work in cell culture and molecular biology at the University of Crete.

Research Interests

Genomic foundation models

Evaluating whether genomic language models generate biologically realistic DNA: k-mer spectra, non-B DNA motifs, CpG islands, TF binding sites and repeat content.

Efficient inference

Speculative decoding and calibration-free compression that make large sequence models faster and able to use million-token contexts on a single GPU.

Interpretability

Understanding the internal mechanisms of text and genomic foundation models, and separating structural signals from causal ones.

Reliable biomedical AI

Measuring the repeated-run reliability of biomedical AI agents and designing selective deferral so they know when not to answer.

Genome statistics at scale

Studying DNA "words" across more than 200,000 genomes, including vocabulary growth (Heaps' law) and strand asymmetries in k-mer usage.

Generative models for science

VAEs and LLMs for molecular and materials design, and deep learning for 3D medical image segmentation.
